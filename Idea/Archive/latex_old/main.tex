\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in Generative Recommendation by capturing complex semantic dependencies between items. However, applying LLMs to real-world user interaction sequences exposes a fundamental architectural flaw: standard positional encodings, such as Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), assign uniformly spaced integer indices to tokens. This "uniform spacing fallacy" renders the attention mechanism blind to the continuous, highly asynchronous physical time gaps between user actions, failing to natively capture critical dynamics like seasonality and interest decay. Conventional solutions either discretize time into tokens, losing continuous granularity, or employ Temporal Point Processes (TPPs) which suffer from intractable integrals over large item vocabularies. 

In this paper, we propose ChronoRoPE, a novel architectural modification that injects continuous physical timestamps directly into the rotational core of the Transformer's attention mechanism. By replacing discrete integer indices with a learnable, lightweight time-warping multi-layer perceptron (MLP), ChronoRoPE dynamically scales the rotation angles of queries and keys based on real-world time intervals. This allows the dot-product attention to naturally decay or align based on temporal distance without expanding the vocabulary or altering the LLM's core dimensionality. Extensive experiments on the Amazon Clothing dataset demonstrate that ChronoRoPE significantly outperforms both traditional sequence models and standard LLM-based generative recommenders, providing an elegant, highly feasible, and computationally efficient solution to continuous-time generative recommendation.
\end{abstract}
